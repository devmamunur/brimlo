## 1. Title formulas: game name (search) vs concept-first curiosity (browse)

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous public data comparing "game name in title" with "concept-first title" for small gaming channels. The SEO-blog consensus is to include the game name when people are likely to search for it, and to put it early. The leading channel in this niche (Idle Cub) seems to rely mostly on concept-first curiosity titles. Inference: a hybrid fits a new channel best. Use a concept-first hook and add the game name after a separator or in the description and tags, especially for brand-new releases where the game name has search demand but few videos.

### Cited Findings
- Idle Cub is a real creator: a German living in Canada, "Playing mostly bullet heaven, incremental, action roguelike, and tower defense type of games." — [Twitch: idle_cub](https://www.twitch.tv/idle_cub)
- A Social Blade snapshot (mid-2026) listed Idle Cub at about 176K subscribers and 86M total views, with the channel created August 22, 2022. The brief says 194K, which is consistent with growth since then. — [Social Blade (search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch)](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/idlecub)
- Example Idle Cub title found independently: "I Found an Incremental Summoner Game and It's Great!" This is a concept-first, first-person discovery framing with no game name. — [Idle Cub YouTube posts (via search snippet)](https://www.youtube.com/@idlecub/posts)
- Idle Cub's tracked coverage (22 games on one aggregator) includes Dwarf Eats Mountain (multi-part series, called "a contender for best incremental of 2026"), Starvester (space-factory incremental, "harvesting stars"), Tower Wizard (first look at a "highly anticipated launch"), Gamblers Table ("most-wishlisted incremental at launch"), Fish Lab, Dirt Clicker, Fortune Mill, Far Mining, Just Keep Fishing, Bones and Coins, My Fire Is Bigger Than Yours, FEED THE AI, Zad Archery ("Three hours from noob to master archer"). About 77% are labeled Incremental, the rest Clicker, Auto-battler, or Idle RPG. Blurbs are reaction-led ("REALLY GOOD", "SO addictive", "most satisfying"). — [Top Incremental Games: Idle Cub creator page](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/creators/idlecub); [Top 10 Incremental & Idle Games, June 2026](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/guides/top-10-incremental-idle-games-june-2026)
- The brief's Idle Cub titles (unverified by me) follow these patterns:
  - "A Game About [verb-ing] [thing] and It's SO SATISFYING!" (concept plus emotional payoff)
  - "[Familiar genre], but with a [twist]!" ("Mining, but with a Skill Tree!")
  - "I Spent [time] Upgrading [weak thing] to [big goal]!" (time investment plus transformation)
  - "Upgrading from [tiny] to [UNSTOPPABLE huge]!" (before to after progression)
  - "This could be one of the BEST Incremental Games of the Year!" (superlative or authority; the brief says this one drew 953K views)
- Generic gaming-title guidance (SEO blog, not data): include the game name "when it helps viewers understand the topic or when people are likely to search for that game," especially for tutorials, reviews, walkthroughs, updates and beginner guides. — [StoryLab: Gaming YouTube title examples](https://storylab.ai/examples/gaming-youtube-video-title-examples/)
- Another blog says to put the game name in the first few words for faster algorithm and viewer pickup, and to search the game name on YouTube to study the top results before finalizing. Low-authority source. — [ytzolo: Viral title for YouTube gaming channel 2026](https://ytzolo.com/blog/viral-title-for-youtube-gaming-channel/)
- YouTube Test & Compare now supports **titles** (added late 2025) as well as thumbnails and title+thumbnail combinations. It allows up to 3 variants and picks the winner by **watch-time share, not CTR**, typically within 1–2 weeks (up to 14 days). — [thumbnailtest.com: Title Test & Compare](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/); [vidanalyze 2026 guide](https://vidanalyze.com/news/youtube-ab-testing-for-titles-thumbnails-2026-guide)
- Sources conflict on Test & Compare eligibility. One says it requires YPP plus Advanced Features; another says no YPP is needed. Both are vendor sources, so check in YouTube Studio. — [thumbnailtest.com: types of testing](https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/types-of-youtube-testing/); [vidanalyze](https://vidanalyze.com/news/youtube-ab-testing-for-titles-thumbnails-2026-guide)

### Inferences
- Concept-first titles work for Idle Cub partly because 176K+ subscribers and an established browse audience already supply impressions. A 0-subscriber channel gets little browse traffic at first. Early on, search for new or obscure Steam incremental titles (low competition, real intent) is one of the few traffic sources a tiny channel can win. Hybrid template: **"[Concept-first hook]! | [Game Name]"** or **"[Game Name]: I Upgraded a Single Goblin into an UNSTOPPABLE Army"**. Run Test & Compare (if eligible) on name-first versus concept-only.
- When to drop the game name: for very obscure games with no search volume, the name wastes characters. When to lead with it: for hyped launches (most-wishlisted, Next Fest standouts, big updates) where people search the name in launch week.
- Title formulas worth reusing, adapted to Brimlo's slower, calm commentary style: "From [tiny] to [huge]", "I Spent 2 Hours Turning [X] into [Y]", "[Genre] but [twist]", "This Incremental Game Got Out of Hand", "A Game About [verb-ing] [noun]…". Avoid copying Idle Cub's exact phrasings so the channel isn't flagged as derivative.
- Use only English that is short and very simple. Caps on one or two power words (SATISFYING, UNSTOPPABLE, BILLIONS) match the niche.

### Gaps
- I found no public A/B data or case study on game-name versus no-game-name titles for small gaming channels. Paddy Galloway-style analyses did not surface in search.
- I could not retrieve per-video views for Idle Cub to check which formula performs best (fetching YouTube and Social Blade failed). The vidIQ MCP was off-limits by instruction.

## 2. Thumbnail patterns

### Takeaway
No niche-specific thumbnail study turned up. The evidence-backed point is to A/B test thumbnails with YouTube Test & Compare, which optimizes for watch-time share. Everything else here is inference from genre conventions.

### Cited Findings
- Test & Compare supports thumbnail tests and picks winners by watch-time share. The point is that a thumbnail promising something the video doesn't deliver loses even with higher CTR. — [thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/)
- Idle Cub's coverage blurbs show the content promise is transformation or escalation: "spoon to the center of the world", "campfire bigger than the Sun", "fighting 100,000 cute animals", "dart to gatling gun". — [Top Incremental Games: Idle Cub](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/creators/idlecub)

### Inferences (not sourced; flag for the writer)
- Best fit for the niche: a **before/after split** (weak start on the left, maxed late game on the right, with an arrow), a **giant number** (e.g., "1 → 1,000,000" or "e308"), or one **hero object grown huge**. These match the escalation promise in the titles and blurbs above.
- Faceless is fine. The incremental niche is gameplay-led and Brimlo is faceless. A small mascot or brand element in gold on navy (#FFC83D→#FF8A00 on #0B1023) can serve as a consistent "face".
- Use real in-game screenshots (late-game, maxed state) for honesty and watch-time share, plus 2–4 words of text at most. Don't repeat the title.
- Teal (#2EE6D6) contrasting with gold works well for numbers and arrows. Check readability at mobile size.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced data on faces versus faceless or numbers versus no numbers for this niche.

## 3. Video structure: hook, chapters, speeding up idle stretches, optimal length

### Takeaway
YouTube's chapter rules are confirmed. I found no niche-specific retention data for 1.5–2.5 hour incremental videos. Structure guidance below is inference, informed by the brief's reference titles ("I Spent 2 Hours…") and one developer-side observation that viewers decide within the first five minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Manual chapter rules: first timestamp 00:00, at least 3 timestamps in ascending order, each chapter at least 10 seconds. Automatic chapters are on by default and manual chapters override them. — [YouTube Help: Video chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)
- A developer-marketing guide notes "viewers often decide within the first five minutes" and recommends press/creator builds skip long unskippable intros. This is evidence that the opening minutes matter for gameplay content. — [Bugnet: how to get streamers to play your game](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-get-streamers-to-play-your-game)
- Idle Cub's long-session framing: "Three hours from noob to master archer" (Zad Archery), and multi-part series for deep games (Dwarf Eats Mountain). — [Top Incremental Games: Idle Cub](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/creators/idlecub)

### Inferences (recommended structure for Brimlo)
- **0:00–0:30 cold open:** 5–15 seconds of the late-game payoff (screen full of numbers or army), then one simple line: "This started with one goblin. Two hours later… this. Let's see how." That promises the before/after arc the title sold.
- **0:30–1:00:** a one-sentence game premise plus what makes it special, then straight into play. No channel intro. Put any "Is it worth buying?" verdict later, as a mid-video or end payoff.
- **Body:** treat each upgrade tier, prestige or rebirth as a chapter, labeled by milestone ("First Prestige", "Unlocking Automation", "1 Million Gold") so chapters double as progress beats. Aim for a visible new mechanic or number jump every few minutes.
- **Idle stretches:** speed-ramp waiting sections (4x–16x) with a small on-screen "x8" label and music, or hard-cut with "20 minutes later…". Keep commentary at normal speed. This turns a 3–4 hour recording into 1.5–2 hours and suits a calmer speaker who talks only when needed.
- **Ending:** a verdict (rating, who it's for, price) plus an end screen to the next similar game.
- **Length for a new channel:** start around 45–90 minutes, which is faster to edit and easier to retain, and move to 1.5–2h once retention graphs show the audience stays. Inference only, with no source found.

### Gaps
- I found no public retention benchmarks for 1–2.5 hour gaming videos or for incremental content specifically.
- I found no sourced data on whether speed-ups hurt or help watch time in this niche.

## 4. Finding games early and getting keys

### Takeaway
Steam Next Fest (three editions per year; next one Oct 19–26, 2026), new-release and upcoming lists, and developer communities are the main early-discovery sources. Key platforms (Keymailer, Woovit, Lurkit) gate access per campaign, so tiny channels are often filtered out. Direct, specific developer email works better for small creators, and devs say fit beats size.

### Cited Findings
- **Next Fest 2026:** Feb 23–Mar 2 and June 15–22 (both over), and **October 19–26, 2026** (dev registration closed Aug 31). These run 10am Pacific. — [Steam Page Analyzer: Next Fest 2026 dates](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-next-fest-2026-dates); [Steamworks: Next Fest June 2026](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026june); [Notebookcheck: H1 2027 schedule, confirms Oct 19–26](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Valve-announces-Steam-Sales-and-Festivals-for-first-half-of-2027.1344181.0.html)
- **Steam incremental tag leaders** (Steam250, Oct 2026), recent entries in the top 40:
  - 2025: Bongo Cat (#1), Shelldiver (#5), Tower Wizard (#15), Leaf it Alone (#22), Space Rock Breaker (#32), Keep on Mining! (#36)
  - 2026: Berry Bury Berry (#9), Bills Must Be Paid (#10), Scritchy Scratchy (#17), Silly Linguine Cat Simulator Deluxe Online (#29)
  - Also high: Nodebuster (2024, #6), Magic Archery (2024, #8), Digseum (2024), Farmer Against Potatoes Idle (2024)
  - [Steam250: Top Incremental](https://steam250.com/tag/incremental)
- **Keymailer:** free for creators. You connect YouTube or Twitch, and a manual accreditation process hand-checks that channels are active, regularly make gaming content and tag correctly. — [hlplanet Keymailer review](https://www.hlplanet.com/is-keymailer-legit-comprehensive-review/). Publishers vet with signals like a "Creator Reliability Score" and predicted views rather than raw follower counts. — [Creator-Hero: Keymailer review](https://www.creator-hero.com/blog/keymailer-pricing-and-review)
- **Per-campaign criteria:** developers set channel size, genre and region filters per campaign. In a 2019 anecdote, a Woovit campaign required Twitter verification and 200 followers, and a creator complained it excluded them. Dated. — [Steam forum (2019)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/729370/discussions/0/3300476731141540729)
- CreatorDB runs an opt-in roster matching pre-release keys to creators by genre. Its FAQ notes that on key-request platforms "anyone can ask and most get ignored." — [CreatorDB keydrop roster](https://creatordb.app/steam-key-drops-for-creators/)
- **Developers' view on targeting:** build target lists from creators who already covered similar games. "A 2,000-viewer streamer whose community lives in your genre" can deliver more wishlists than a 100K generalist. Smaller creators "read email, respond, and remember developers." Devs use demo launches and festivals as the "reason-why-now". — [Bugnet: get streamers to play your game](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-get-streamers-to-play-your-game)
- A Jan 2026 Reddit post by an incremental developer says even small creator coverage "can actually move wishlists". The source is a mirror and the subreddit is unconfirmed, so treat it as a lead. — [Reddit snapshot mirror](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qkquiq/snapshots/2026-01-24T02%3A51%3A22.76091Z)
- **Key-scam context:** devs are wary because a person posing as a 50K-sub YouTuber scammed about 25% of contacted developers with a single free email. Devs now verify active channels, relevant audience and history of game content. — [PC Invasion](https://pcinvasion.com/?p=87086); [Bugnet: send Steam keys safely](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-send-steam-keys-to-streamers-safely)

### Inferences (practical workflow)
- **Weekly scouting (about 30 min):**
  1. Steam "Upcoming" and "New & Trending" filtered by the Incremental, Idler and Clicker tags.
  2. SteamDB for upcoming release dates and follower or wishlist-proxy growth.
  3. r/incremental_games, where devs post demos and releases.
  4. itch.io incremental tag for prototypes that later come to Steam.
  5. Top Incremental Games' new-release pages.
  6. Idle Cub's Discord list and similar creators' recent uploads, to see what is getting traction. Cover games they haven't touched, or cover them first.
- **Be early:** play demos during Next Fest (Oct 19–26, 2026 is the next one, during week 2 of the Brimlo plan), then follow those games for a launch-day video. Wishlist and follow promising demos so Steam notifies you at launch.
- **Getting keys as a tiny channel:** first build 5–10 videos of relevant incremental coverage, because devs and platforms check for history. Then email devs directly via the Steam page or press kit, stating the specific game fit and linking your best incremental video. Say you'll cover the demo and launch, and ask for nothing else. Use the channel's business email, never request keys in bulk, and be transparent about channel size. Register on Keymailer early, since it's free and manual accreditation takes time.

### Gaps
- I couldn't confirm current Woovit or Lurkit minimum requirements (sites not fetched; criteria are per campaign).
- I found no data on Keymailer approval odds for channels under 1K subscribers.

## 5. Which game types are performing best in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Steam's top-rated incremental titles in 2025–2026 are dominated by small, cheap, "satisfying" mining, digging, harvesting and number-go-up games (Shelldiver, Tower Wizard, Keep on Mining!, Space Rock Breaker, Berry Bury Berry, Bills Must Be Paid) plus desktop-companion idlers (Bongo Cat). Incremental tower defense and horde (survivors-like) hybrids are a clear sub-trend. Leading creator coverage centres on mining, fishing, factory and escalation themes.

### Cited Findings
- Steam250 incremental ranking (2025–26 entries listed in section 4) shows mining and digging (Keep on Mining!, Space Rock Breaker, Digseum), tower or wizard (Tower Wizard) and idle-companion (Bongo Cat) styles at the top. — [Steam250](https://steam250.com/tag/incremental)
- Incremental tower defense on Steam:
  - Sir, We Have an Orc Problem ("tens of thousands of orcs with a massive incremental upgrade tree", 87%)
  - Outhold (86%)
  - Zero Stress King: Idle Defense (88%)
  - [Top Incremental Games: Tower defense](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/category/tower-defense)
- Sept 2026 idler list includes Orc Incremental (auto-battler with rebirths) and Bones and Coins (dungeon crawler with hero party), both with modest ratings around 7.0–7.3. — [wasdland: Best Idler Games, Sept 2026](https://www.wasdland.com/games/idler/)
- Idle Cub's 2026 coverage is heavy on mining (Dwarf Eats Mountain, Far Mining, Dirt Clicker), fishing (Fish Lab, Just Keep Fishing, Far Fishing), factory or space (Starvester) and horde escalation (shatAAAAp!, 100,000 animals). — [Top Incremental Games: Idle Cub](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/creators/idlecub)

### Inferences
- The themes Idle Cub returns to most (mining, digging, fishing, horde or army scaling, tower defense with upgrade trees) are a safe core for Brimlo. They thumbnail well (small to huge) and play well in 1–2h sessions because a full run often fits in a single sitting.
- Desktop-companion idlers like Bongo Cat rank high on Steam but probably make poor 2-hour videos (little on-screen progression). Treat them as a low priority for long-form.

## 6. Mac (Apple silicon) and CrossOver compatibility

### Takeaway
Check Steam's macOS icon first. For Windows-only games, use the CodeWeavers compatibility database, AppleGamingWiki and MacGamingDB before committing a recording day. Small 2D incremental games (Unity, Godot, GameMaker) are usually low-demand, but anti-cheat titles fail. CrossOver has a 14-day full trial.

### Cited Findings
- The CodeWeavers compatibility database lists 22,710 applications (4,627 gold medal), filterable by rating and platform (Mac/Linux). Steam is the most-ranked app (47,608 ranks) and most-downloaded CrossTie. CrossOver offers a "14-day, completely functional trial." — [CodeWeavers compatibility](https://www.codeweavers.com/compatibility)
- CodeWeavers' recommended method: install Steam through CrossOver (its CrossTie), then install games into that Steam bottle. Some games have game-specific CrossTies with extra fixes. — [CodeWeavers blog (2023)](https://codeweavers.com/blog/mjohnson/2023/3/8/three-game-clients-that-work-on-macos-with-crossover)
- Community-recommended Mac compatibility databases: codeweavers.com/compatibility, applegamingwiki.com and macgamingdb.app. AppleGamingWiki also lists per-game settings. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/2/524225618711431017/)
- Games with aggressive anti-cheat (e.g., PUBG, Apex) won't work in CrossOver. — [MacGamerHQ CrossOver guide](https://macgamerhq.com/virtualization/windows-games-crossover)
- Apple's Game Porting Toolkit (D3DMetal) builds on CrossOver's source and translates x86 to Arm and DirectX 12 to Metal, with expected performance loss. It is aimed at developers, while CrossOver is for end users. (2023 source.) — [Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.ch/en/page/windows-games-auf-mac-klappt-das-jetzt-28412)
- Older community reports (2021) call CrossOver "hit or miss". These are dated and predate D3DMetal. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/2/524225618711431017/)

### Inferences
- Test every Windows-only game in CrossOver on a weekday evening (10 minutes: launch, settings, one upgrade loop, OBS capture check) before the weekend recording day. Keep a backup Mac-native game queued.
- OBS capture of CrossOver windows: use macOS Screen Capture (display or window). Expect higher CPU load with 16GB RAM, so close other apps.
- Demos usually ship the same platform builds as the full game, so test the demo to see whether the launch build will run.

### Gaps
- I didn't fetch current CrossOver version and Apple silicon specifics for 2026, or any incremental-game-specific compatibility list.

## 7. Light-editing workflow (OBS markers, silence removal, DaVinci and CapCut, 2026)

### Takeaway
OBS (30.2+) can drop chapter markers by hotkey into Hybrid MP4 recordings, and they show in DaVinci Resolve. That gives a fast way to tag "upgrade moments" for chapters and cuts. For silence removal, one Oct 2026 source says Resolve (20.2+/21) Free has "Ripple Delete Silence". CapCut desktop's auto silence removal is a Pro feature per most sources.

### Cited Findings
- OBS Hybrid MP4 (added in OBS 30.2) supports chapter markers added by the "Add chapter marker" hotkey, WebSocket, or frontend API, named "Unnamed <N>". The docs say most editors, including DaVinci Resolve, support them. **Chapters are written at finalization, so they are lost if OBS crashes.** — [OBS KB: Hybrid MP4](https://obsproject.com/kb/hybrid-mp4)
- One user reported markers landing about 65 seconds late in Resolve with a variable offset. Test before relying on them. — [OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/add-chapter-marker-not-at-correct-spot-in-clip.180279/)
- "Split Recording File" and "Add Chapter Marker" are different hotkeys. — [OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/how-to-use-obs-hotkey-split-recording-file.181420/)
- A third-party OBS plugin (StreamUP Chapter Marker Manager) adds named markers and export. — [OBS resources](https://obsproject.com/forum/resources/streamup-chapter-marker-manager.1962/update/8064/)
- **DaVinci Resolve silence removal (single, vendor source, updated Oct 2026):** in Resolve 21 (requires 20.2+), select a clip in the Edit or Cut page, then **Clip > Audio Operations > Ripple Delete Silence**, available in Free and Studio. Fairlight Remove Silence and transcript-based removal are Studio-only. Older articles say Free has no native silence removal, so they conflict and are probably outdated. — [Premiere Copilot: remove silence in Resolve (2026)](https://www.premierecopilot.com/en/blog/remove-silence-davinci-resolve); paid plugins such as FireCut also work with Resolve 20+ Free — [FireCut](https://firecut.ai/features/davinci-resolve/remove-silences)
- **CapCut desktop:** auto silence removal requires CapCut Pro (from about $7.99/month per one blog). A competitor blog claims it's free. Sources conflict, so check CapCut's pricing page. — [Blitzcut: CapCut remove silence 2026](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/capcut-remove-silence); contradicted by [Cutsio](https://cutsio.com/blog/capcut-silence-remover-vs-cutsio-comparison)

### Inferences (workflow)
- **Record:**
  1. OBS, Hybrid MP4, mic on a separate audio track.
  2. Press the chapter-marker hotkey at every milestone: new mechanic, prestige, big number, funny moment.
  3. Keep a paper or notes list of "marker N = what".
  4. Stop the recording cleanly (no crash) so markers are written.
- **Edit in Resolve Free (one day):**
  1. Import, then jump marker to marker.
  2. Cut dead setup and menus.
  3. Speed-ramp idle stretches (Retime, Change Clip Speed) and add an "x8" text overlay.
  4. Use Ripple Delete Silence only on pure-silence sections. Don't apply it globally to gameplay, because the gameplay itself has audio.
  5. Convert markers to YouTube timestamps (Resolve can export the timeline marker list as EDL or CSV; then map it to 00:00 format).
- Gameplay continuity matters more than tightening commentary in this niche. Speed-ups beat jump-cuts during idle play.

### Gaps
- I didn't confirm from Blackmagic's own docs that "Ripple Delete Silence" is in Resolve Free (single vendor source).
- I didn't verify current CapCut Pro pricing or region availability in Bangladesh.
