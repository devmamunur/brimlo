# Catch New Games Early, Post More Often

The channels most like Idle Cub are **Dex** (Germany, 312K subscribers), **MOREQUIS** (US, 62.9K) and **Sifd** (Greece, 148K). The best models for a new channel like Brimlo are two 2025–2026 starters: **CG Grows** and **Nerfed Reflexes**. Your format is fine. Long videos of 1–2 hours grow in this niche. Calm, non-native English is fine too, because three of the biggest channels are run by a German and a Greek. Two parts of your plan are weak. First, volume. Every small incremental channel that is growing fast posts **4–7 long videos per week**. You plan 2, which is about one third of that. Second, timing. The biggest breakouts on small channels in the last 90 days came from videos posted **within 1–10 days** of a game's demo, playtest or launch. Fixed Tuesday/Friday slots will sometimes make you late. The money path is also harder than before. You cannot reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours before **1 February 2027**, so plan for the new **8,000-hour** bar. My math shows that one breakout video with about 36,000 views can supply most of those 8,000 hours by itself. So the strategy is simple: post more chances, post them early on new games, and package each one with a clear "tiny to huge" promise. Your first action is this week. The **I Am Plague** playtest closes on **19 October**, and Steam Next Fest runs **19–26 October 2026**.

**How to read this report.** A fact has a source link after it. vidIQ data has no public link, so I link to the channel or video and write "vidIQ" with the date (12–13 Oct 2026). vidIQ numbers are estimates. A sentence marked **[Inference]** is my own judgment from the data, not a sourced fact.

## Growing small channels post 4–7 videos a week; your 2 is too few

This is the most important challenge to your plan. In the data, every fast-growing small incremental channel posted **15–24 long videos in 30 days**. That is 4–7 per week. Azrilich: 23. CG Grows: 21. Nerfed Reflexes: 21. Idle Gamer: 24. FungalBrainRot: 37. The researchers found **no incremental channel growing at only 1–3 videos per week** (vidIQ, 12 Oct 2026, links in the table above). The only slow-posting growers were in other genres, like Typical Tea's calm city-builder videos at about 1 per week ([vidIQ](https://www.youtube.com/@typicalteagames)), or heavily edited "challenge" channels like Codz, which has 39.8K subscribers from only 14 videos ([vidIQ](https://www.youtube.com/@itscodz)).

Why does volume matter so much? Because growth here comes from hits. In every fast-growing channel, 1–3 videos did 10–40 times better than the channel's normal video. CG Grows' parasite video got 551K when its normal video gets 10K–30K. Nerfed Reflexes had two videos at about 350K each ([vidIQ, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.youtube.com/@nerfedreflexes)). [Inference] You cannot predict which video will hit. More good videos means more chances.

But volume alone also fails. Many new channels have hundreds of videos and almost no subscribers. Gaming Ghost has 444 videos and 132 subscribers ([vidIQ](https://www.youtube.com/@gamingghostgoblin)). PirroGaming has 275 videos and 165 subscribers ([vidIQ](https://www.youtube.com/@mrpirrogaming)). [Inference] Volume only helps when each video has a good game, a good title and a good thumbnail.

There is also a real limit: your full-time job. Creator surveys report burnout for **52%** of creators ([Billion Dollar Boy](https://www.billiondollarboy.com/news/over-half-of-creators-face-burnout)) and **62%** in a study of 542 full- and part-time creators ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=189316)). The top causes are creative fatigue and workload. If you quit after three months, you get nothing. So I do not recommend jumping to 5 per week.

**My recommendation [Inference]: start at 2 per week, move to 3 per week by week 5, and test 4 per week after week 12 if your energy allows.** Get the extra video from the same recording time, not more recording time. Your 3–4 hour session can produce either one 1.5–2h video or two shorter videos of 60–90 minutes on two different games. Both length bands work. The growing channels cluster at **30–45 minutes** (Azrilich, Nerfed Reflexes) and **75–120 minutes** (CG Grows, Idle Gamer, MOREQUIS, Dex). In the vidIQ outlier list, 30 of 40 breakout videos were 40 minutes or longer, and several broke out at 83–112 minutes ([vidIQ, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g4THPD6AhKI)). So length is not your limit. Number of chances is.

Do not make the second video a "Part 2" of the same game. That is the Idle Niv trap. Make it a different game.

**Upload time.** The notes contain **no data** on the best upload day or hour for this niche. Tuesday and Friday at 12 PM US Eastern is a reasonable choice. It is 10 PM in Dhaka now. [Fact, general calendar] US clocks move back on Sunday 1 November 2026, so from then it will be 11 PM in Dhaka. Use YouTube Studio's schedule feature and it will handle this. [Inference] Being first on a new game matters more than the exact hour, as the next section shows.

A thumbnail that promises something the video does not show will lose, because YouTube's Test & Compare picks the winner by **watch-time share, not click rate**, and now tests titles too, with up to 3 versions ([thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/)). [Inference] This is misleading metadata, its benefit is not proven, and it can look like spam. Tag the game name, the genre and your own channel name only.

## The 8,000-hour bar applies to you, and one hit can cover most of it

**The rule change is official.** YouTube's blog, 10 August 2026: "New creators applying for YPP will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days." It starts **1 February 2027**, and creators already in YPP are not affected ([YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)). Until then, the bar is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)). Outside coverage says the 1,000-subscriber rule stays ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-requirements-2027)).

**Be honest: you will not make the old bar.** From today there are about 16 weeks until 1 February 2027. That is about 32 videos at 2 per week. To get 4,000 hours by then, each video would need about **555 views** with about 13.5 minutes watched per view, and you would also need 1,000 subscribers. For a brand-new channel this is very unlikely. Plan for **8,000 hours**.

**The lower "expanded" tier is not open to you.** It gives memberships, Super Thanks and Super Chat at 500 subscribers, but **Bangladesh is not on its country list** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en)). The standard YPP is available in Bangladesh ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7101720)). [Inference] So you get no fan money until full YPP. Confirm this in your Studio "Earn" tab.

**Watch-hour math.** 8,000 hours is 480,000 minutes. A CreatorDB study models viewers watching about **15% of videos over one hour** ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/research/youtube-8000-hour-rule/)). This is a model, not measured data. With that number:

| Your video length | Minutes watched per view (15%) | Views needed in 365 days for 8,000 h | Per video, at 104 videos/year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 min | 13.5 min | about 35,600 | about 340 |
| 120 min | 18 min | about 26,700 | about 255 |

[Inference, my arithmetic] Now look at the outliers. **One video with 36,000 views at a 90-minute length gives about 8,000 hours by itself.** CG Grows' 551K video or Haikrazon's 145K video would each pass the bar many times over, if viewers watch that much. That is why this report pushes you toward new games and more chances. YPP in this niche is mostly a "one hit" problem, plus 1,000 subscribers. Long videos help you here, because each view gives more hours.

**How long will it take?** No good data exists for new gaming channels. Blogs say "6–12 months" for 4,000 hours, with no data ([tubemilestone](https://tubemilestone.com/blog/how-long-to-get-4000-watch-hours)). CreatorDB found that the median active channel with 1,000+ subscribers makes only about **3,400 long-form hours per year**, and only **43%** of them clear 8,000 ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/research/youtube-8000-hour-rule/)). [Inference] Without a hit, expect 12–18 months or more. With one real hit, it can happen in 3–6 months. Plan your energy for the long case.

**Shorts and live streams.** Views in the Shorts feed **do not count** toward watch hours. Public live streams that stay up as public videos **do count** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)). Shorts earn very little, about $0.03–0.08 per 1,000 views by third-party estimates ([Eklipse](https://blog.eklipse.gg/basic-knowledge/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-gaming-which-grows-faster.html)). [Inference] Shorts are optional, for discovery only. Live streaming has a time-zone problem: US evening prime time is about 5–8 AM in Dhaka. Skip streaming for the first 12 weeks.

**Policy risk: silence is the danger, not your accent.** YouTube says video-game videos can earn when the "commentary is strictly tied to the live action being shown", but "videos simply showing a user playing a video game… for extended periods of time may not be accepted for monetization" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/138161?hl=en)). The inauthentic-content rule targets repetitive videos with "minimal… commentary" or "minimal variation across videos", while a series is fine when "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). Your "commentary when needed" style has one risk: long idle waits with no talking. [Inference] Speed up or cut every idle wait. Keep talking during the parts you keep. Give every video a clear, different goal. YouTube gives no number for "enough" commentary. Some publishers allow all commercial use of their games ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/138161?hl=en)). [Inference] Check each indie game's video policy on its Steam page or press kit.

**What the money looks like.** Mid-roll ads are allowed on videos of 8 minutes or more, and YouTube places them at natural breaks by default ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6175006?hl=en)). Third-party estimates put long-form gaming RPM (your earnings per 1,000 views) with US viewers at about **$2–6, median about $3.50** ([ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/youtube-earnings/gaming/united-states); [ytmoneycalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/average-youtube-rpm/)). Gaming is one of the lowest-paying major niches. [Inference] 10,000 views a month is about $35. 100,000 views a month is about $350. Long videos help watch hours more than they help RPM, because most viewers leave before the later ads. vidIQ estimates Azrilich at about $17.5K per month ([vidIQ](https://www.youtube.com/@azrilichidle)), but that is an unverified estimate for a 61.7K channel with about 109K views per video. Paid sponsorships below 1,000 subscribers are unlikely. Key platforms like Keymailer and Lurkit mostly give free keys, and no public pay rates exist ([Lurkit case study](https://case-studies.lurkit.com/versus-evil)).

**Bangladesh payout and tax.** AdSense pays Bangladesh by **wire transfer**, not EFT ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14728152?hl=en)). YouTube treats YPP income as "Other Copyright Royalties". US tax is taken only on earnings from US viewers, at 0–30% depending on your treaty status. If you give no tax info, YouTube can hold back up to **24% of all your earnings worldwide** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en)). The US–Bangladesh tax treaty caps royalty tax at **10%** ([US Treaty Doc 109-05](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CDOC-109tdoc5/pdf/CDOC-109tdoc5.pdf)). [Inference] Submit a W-8BEN as an individual and claim the treaty. Whether the AdSense form shows exactly 10% for Bangladesh is not verified. On the Bangladesh side, banks have deducted 7.5% on foreign service income, and the FY2026-27 budget proposed removing this for content creators ([Daily Observer](https://observerbd.com/news/578567)). The Finance Minister said creators could bring in up to $5,000 without paperwork ([UNB](https://unb.com.bd/category/Bangladesh/budget-allows-freelancers-content-creators-to-remit-5000-without-paperwork-minister/188852)). Whether these became law is not verified. One more rule: YouTube may turn off monetization on channels inactive for 6 months or more ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is not "Is my format good enough?" Your format is proven: Dex, MOREQUIS and CG Grows all grow with 1–2 hour single-game videos, and non-native creators lead this niche. The real question is "How many good chances can I make each month, and how fast?" The 8,000-hour rule sounds scary, but in a niche where a 1.5-hour video can get 100K+ views on a 2K-subscriber channel, it is mostly a question of getting one or two hits. Hits come from new games, posted early, with a clear "tiny to huge" promise in the title and thumbnail.

So your two biggest risks are not your English or your Mac. They are posting too little, and posting too late because of fixed slots. Keep Tuesday and Friday as your base. Allow a "fast lane" upload when a demo or playtest is hot. Grow to 3 videos per week by getting two videos from one session. Protect your energy, because a channel that stops at week 10 gets nothing. Nobody in the data was found growing in this niche at 2 per week, so treat your first 12 weeks as an experiment. Measure, and then decide.

### 2. Recording and talking rules

1. Use OBS with **Hybrid MP4** (OBS 30.2 or newer). Set a hotkey for "Add chapter marker" and press it at every milestone: a new mechanic, a prestige, a big number, a funny moment. Stop the recording cleanly, because markers are lost if OBS crashes ([OBS KB](https://obsproject.com/kb/hybrid-mp4)). Test the marker timing once, because one user reported markers about 65 seconds late ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/add-chapter-marker-not-at-correct-spot-in-clip.180279/)).
2. Put your mic on its own audio track.
3. **Talk about what is on screen.** Simple lines are enough, and slow English is fine: "Now I buy this upgrade, because…", "Before, I made 10 per second. Now I make 1,000.", "Next, I want to unlock…", "This part is boring, so let's speed up."
4. **Never leave long silent idle stretches in the final video.** Speed them up 4x–16x with a small "x8" label, or cut with "20 minutes later…". Silence plus waiting is the main policy risk.
5. **First 30 seconds:** show 5–15 seconds of the late-game result, then one line: "This started with one rat. Two hours later… this. Let's see how." Then the game idea in one sentence, then play. No channel intro. A developer guide notes viewers often decide in the first five minutes ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-get-streamers-to-play-your-game)).
6. **Chapters:** start at 00:00, at least 3 chapters, each at least 10 seconds ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)). Name them by progress: "First Prestige", "Automation", "1 Million Gold".
7. **End:** a short verdict (is it worth it, who is it for, price) and an end screen to your next similar video.
8. **Edit in DaVinci Resolve Free.** Jump from marker to marker. One source says Resolve 21 Free has "Ripple Delete Silence" ([Premiere Copilot](https://www.premierecopilot.com/en/blog/remove-silence-davinci-resolve)). Use it only on silent menu parts, not on gameplay.
9. **Target length:** 60–120 minutes. Shorter is fine when the game is short. Never stretch a video to reach 2 hours.

### 4. Thumbnail rules

1. **One idea only.** Pick one: before → after with an arrow, "0%" vs "100%", one giant number ("160,000 RATS"), or one hero object grown huge.
2. **At most 2–4 words**, or one number. Heavy white or yellow text. Do not repeat the title.
3. **Use real in-game screenshots** from the late game. Do not promise what the video does not show.
4. **Strong contrast:** red vs green, or dark vs bright. Your brand colors work: gold (#FFC83D to #FF8A00) on navy (#0B1023), with teal (#2EE6D6) for numbers and arrows.
5. Add one small, consistent brand mark in the same corner every time. It works as your "face".
6. **Check at phone size.** If you cannot read it at the size of a thumbnail on a phone, cut words.
