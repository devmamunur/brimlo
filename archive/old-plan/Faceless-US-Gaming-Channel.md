Faceless US Gaming Channel — Niche Research (Oct 2026)

Oct 6, 2026 · @Mamunur Rashid

Executive summary

Go with long-form incremental, idle and "satisfying sim" Steam indie games, with your own voice-over. Keep Roblox horror and story games for a 13+ audience as the backup. The key number: Azrilich is a faceless, US-based channel in this niche that started on 11 June 2026. It reached 59K subscribers and 11.1M views in under four months (about 2.9M long-form views a month), with no Shorts at all. CG Grows, another US faceless channel that started on 24 June, posts 1 to 2 hour videos of the same kind of games and its views were up 167% in the last 30 days.

Your $10 RPM target is not realistic from gaming ads alone. US long-form gaming RPM is estimated at $2.40 to $6.40 per 1,000 views (vidIQ), real creator data puts the gaming median at $2.05 (AIR, 300 channels), and gaming Shorts pay about $0.08. The most you can realistically expect is about $5 to $7 earned per 1,000 views, and that needs videos of 20 minutes or more with mid-roll ads, a mostly US/UK/Canada audience, and developer sponsorships on top of ads.

Three catches to plan around:

Mac: most of the small incremental games blowing up right now are Windows-only on Steam, including A Game About Digging a Hole, Keep on Mining!, Scratch the Ticket and Nodebuster. You will need CrossOver (about $74 a year, with a 14-day free trial) or must stick to the Mac-native titles listed in the recommendation.
Timing: the bar for joining the Partner Program doubles to 8,000 watch hours on 1 Feb 2027. Starting now and applying before that date makes a big difference.
Steam Next Fest runs 19 to 26 October 2026. It brings hundreds of free demos, which are exactly what the fastest-growing channels in this niche play.
RPM reality check

Realistic US long-form gaming RPM is $2.40 to $6.40, and Shorts pay about $0.08, roughly a fiftieth of long-form. The single gaming figure above $10 comes from AutonoLab's sample of just five channels, and AIR's 300-channel study, built from real Studio data, puts the gaming median at $2.05. Trust the bigger samples.

Sub-genres: no source splits RPM for cozy, idle, simulation or mobile casual. The only split is Roblox at about $2.20 (AutonoLab, five channels, directional). Kid-heavy Roblox and mobile videos sit lower still, because "Made for Kids" removes personalised ads.
Length and mid-rolls: AIR found a 10-minute video can carry several mid-rolls while an 8-minute one struggles to fit one, and the median monetized view carried 1.55 ads. Videos of 20 to 60 minutes, like Azrilich's and CG Grows', sit at the top of the range.
Tech blend: Mac tech tutorials estimate $3 to $8 (vidIQ), only slightly above gaming, and AIR's real data shows tech and gaming at almost the same median ($2.33 vs $2.05). A Mac angle helps with US share more than with RPM.

What could push earnings toward $10 per 1,000 views (modelled, not measured): ads at $4 on US-heavy 30-minute videos, plus developer sponsorships or paid features worth about $2 per 1,000 views, plus memberships and affiliate links worth about $0.50. That comes to about $6.50. Getting to $10 would need sponsorship rates I could not verify, so treat $5 to $7 as the realistic ceiling for year one.

What the standouts do differently
Azrilich: story-style "I..." titles on tiny new Steam games. Examples: "I cheated the lottery with a FLASHLIGHT" (956K views, Scratch the Ticket) and "I made a fire so big it bullied the sun" (1.15M). The titles promise an absurd result and never use the game's name. Videos run 19 to 65 minutes, with 24 uploads in the last 30 days and many of them free demos.
CG Grows: search-style titles and very long videos. "The parasite incremental game where you infest a planet that FIGHTS BACK" got 515K views at 1 h 14 min. Every title names the genre plus the hook, and descriptions link the Steam page. That helps developers, who then share the video.
Melon and Sunny Games: incremental mechanics inside Roblox. Simulators, tycoons and "+1 every second" games at about 21 minutes. It shows the same "number goes up" hook works with Roblox's audience.
Dabble: one upload every day at 12 PM ET. Cozy indies and roguelikes at about 55 minutes. Steady volume rather than viral hits.
Cam2r Gaming Movies: full indie horror games cut as 2-hour "movies". Seven a week; the longest videos in the sample, which pile up watch hours fast.

None of the long-form winners depend on Shorts. These are winners that vidIQ surfaced, so expect survivorship bias: most channels started in the same months grew far more slowly.

Recommendation and launch plan

Primary: long-form incremental, idle and satisfying-sim Steam indies with your voice. It has the best mix of fast new-channel growth, US-based competitors, an older Steam audience with no Made for Kids risk, and videos long enough for several mid-rolls. Developers of small games also want coverage, which opens the door to free keys and paid features.

Backup: Roblox horror and story games aimed at 13+, following the playbook already in this project. It has the highest US search share (32%) and runs natively on your Mac, but pays less (about $2.20 RPM) and its trends die within weeks.

Mac setup
Install CrossOver's 14-day free trial ($74 a year after that) and run Windows Steam inside it. Test every Windows-only game in the trial before you plan a video around it.
Favour Mac-native games where they fit (marked below). They are free of compatibility risk.
Record with OBS (free, runs natively on Mac) and use a USB microphone.
Skip anything with anti-cheat; CrossOver cannot run those games.

Launch plan
Length: 25 to 45 minutes for most videos, with dead time cut. Add one 90-minute "100%" or relaxing video every two weeks for watch hours.
Frequency: 3 long videos a week (for example Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday). About 15 hours a week alongside your job is my estimate; time yourself in week 1.
Shorts: optional, 2 or 3 a week cut from the long videos. They are a discovery tool, not income; neither Azrilich nor CG Grows posts any.
Upload time: 12 PM US Eastern, which is 10 PM in Dhaka until 1 Nov and 11 PM after US clocks change. Dabble posts daily at 12 PM ET. Schedule uploads in YouTube Studio. This timing is common practice, not something I measured.
Voice: live commentary or voice-over on every video. It is what separates you from "inauthentic" no-commentary uploads.
Audience setting: mark the channel "No, it's not made for kids", and keep thumbnails and language aimed at teens and adults.
Developers: link each game's Steam page in the description and add a business email to the channel. That is how keys and paid features arrive.
Next Fest week (19 to 26 Oct): record five or more demos, publish the round-up first, then the strongest demo as its own video.

Risks and how to reduce them

The biggest risk specific to you is the Mac: the games driving this niche are mostly Windows-only. The rest apply to any new gaming channel.

#	Risk	Evidence	How to reduce it
1	A hot game won't run on your Mac	A Game About Digging a Hole, Keep on Mining!, Scratch the Ticket, Nodebuster and PowerWash Simulator are Windows-only on Steam. Whisky, the free Windows layer, stopped development in 2025.	Use CrossOver's trial to test before you buy it. Keep 2 or 3 tested games in reserve. Lean on native titles (Cookie Clicker, Sandustry, Supermarket Simulator, Cast n Chill, Stardew Valley). Avoid anti-cheat games.
2	Inauthentic or reused content demonetization	YouTube renamed "repetitious" content to "inauthentic" on 15 July 2025, and a January 2026 enforcement wave hit synthetic narration, templated thumbnails and channels uploading at unsustainable rates.	Real voice commentary on every video, no AI voice, edited pacing, varied thumbnails, and a human-scale upload rate (3 to 5 a week).
3	Copyright and publisher rules	Most indie developers welcome videos, but some games include licensed music that can trigger Content ID claims. Roblox's guidelines forbid using the Roblox logo on general creative materials. I could not find a Roblox page that sets out rules for monetized gameplay videos (unverified).	Check each game's Steam page or press kit for a video policy, and mute or replace licensed tracks. Leave the Roblox logo, and branded characters such as Spider-Man, out of thumbnails and channel art.
4	Trends fade and the niche saturates	Searches fell 62% for Sandustry, 69% for Scratch the Ticket and 78% for Cast n Chill after their spikes. "Incremental games" has 55 competition with many new channels piling in.	Be early: use Next Fest, Steam's upcoming-games lists and new demos. Mix in evergreen games (Cookie Clicker, Stardew Valley). Build a recognizable title and thumbnail style so people subscribe to you, not to one game.
5	Made for Kids label	35% of Roblox users who have age-checked are under 13 (Roblox, Q2 2026). Going Balls and obby content skews young. Incremental Steam games carry little of this risk.	Set the channel to "not made for kids". Avoid cartoonish, toy-like thumbnails and child-level language. If you use the Roblox backup, stick to horror and challenge games for teens.

One more thing to watch: new Partner Program applicants need 8,000 watch hours from 1 Feb 2027, double today's 4,000. Long videos are the fastest way to get there.
